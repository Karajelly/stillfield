# How to play

The full guide to Stillfield's controls and farming loop.


- Tap where cultivating, sowing, harvesting, or mowing should finish. The tractor continues until its trailing implement works that square, then stops. In overhead view, drag across the meadow to keep choosing destinations. Pinch or use the zoom buttons to change your view.
- Tap the vehicle button beside the zoom controls (or press **V** in the browser) to switch into **Tractor view**, a perspective view following behind you. Drag to roll: sliding left or right from where you touched swings the tractor toward that direction and holds it there (a slide of about 100 points is a quarter turn), returning to center goes straight, and lifting lets it settle to a stop. A tap that stays put still chooses a work endpoint, however long you hold it. A mouse works the same way. On a keyboard, **W** or **↑** rolls, **A**/**D** or **←**/**→** steer gently and straighten when released, and **S** or **↓** settles to a stop. A quick tap still chooses a work endpoint. Pinch, or use the zoom buttons, to adjust camera distance. Each view keeps its own zoom during play, and your chosen view saves with your meadow.
- With **Freehand** chosen in Settings, after a straight row, let the tractor settle and tap the neighboring ground beside its end. It lifts the implement, turns into your chosen spot, faces back along the row, works the spot, and stops ready for your next input. Freehand has no suggested lanes, spacing correction, or automatic next passes.
- **Tidy rows (the default).** A tap asks for a whole row. The field is divided into lanes one implement wide. The tractor drives to the tapped lane with its implement lifted, then works exactly that lane from where it stood to where you tapped, so rows sit side by side with no gaps and nothing is worked on the way there. Tap more rows whenever you like: they wait their turn, and the tractor moves from one to the next without stopping. A tap in the lane it is on moves that row's end. Once a row is done, a tap back along the same lane turns the tractor round toward the side you tapped. Tap the same spot twice to fill every lane up to it. In the overhead view, a pale wash shows the rows still to come. Tap beyond the field's edge to leave them and drive there with the implement up. Dragging in the overhead view still steers freehand, steering by hand in Tractor view takes over at once, and in the browser's overhead view the arrow keys and WASD settle onto the same lanes. Tidy rows are on until you choose **Freehand** in Settings, and the choice saves with your meadow.
- A cream ring stays on the spot you chose. Tap again to redirect the tractor; its cruising pace stays gentle. The 2.5 m working swath spans five 50 cm cells and follows the visible deck continuously between frames, with 25 cm of forgiving edge coverage on each side.
- A lane runs west past the tree line to **Foxglove field**, a second field twice the size of the first, with its own soil, crops, and harvest display. Tap the signpost button beside the zoom controls to ride there with the implement lifted, or tap the lane and drive it yourself. The view stays still while you work inside a field and glides along with the tractor on the lane, in portrait and landscape alike. Rotating your device keeps the same field in view. The signpost always points to the other end of the lane; tap it again mid-ride to stop, or tap anywhere to take over.
- The main dock follows **Cultivate → Sow → Harvest**, with **Wander** for a quiet ride. Cultivate grass, stubble, or unwanted crops into prepared soil; mowing is optional. Plowing over crops leaves soil ready to sow without adding harvest rewards. Sowing only works in prepared soil. Three illustrated crop buttons appear while **Sow** is selected. Choose wheat, sunflowers, or pumpkins; seeds are unlimited and all crops use the same tools.
- Crops move through planted soil, shoots, leafy growth, and maturity. Sunflowers take **3 minutes**, wheat **6**, and pumpkins **9** of active play. Mature crops wait indefinitely. Pausing, opening a sheet, or leaving the game pauses growth; no time passes offline.
- Harvest mature crops to leave stubble and fill the field’s display by the barn path: sunflower pots, golden wheat sheaves in a wheelbarrow, and pumpkins in a wooden cart. The first harvested cell is visible immediately. Displays fill through three stages and never prevent further harvesting.
- Sowing immediately changes the soil: golden seed rows for wheat, striped seed pairs on olive-brown beds for sunflowers, and simple orange seed rows for pumpkins. Small seeds make fresh planting visible in tractor view as well as from above.
- A new farm starts with three small, mature plots of wheat, sunflowers, and pumpkins, ready to harvest. Two full mown strips and a half-length strip sit along the bottom of the field, with the tractor at the end of the half strip. Repeated sowing and invalid passes leave crops and progress untouched. There is no selling, hauling, upkeep, or penalty for stopping.
- The pause button gives you a moment to breathe.
- Settings control pace, the time of day, haptics, and nature-sound volume. The tractor cruises at 3 m/s by default; the pace slider reaches 4.5 m/s.
- Enable sound with the speaker button for wind, birds, and a soft tractor hum.
- **The day drifts by.** Morning, a long afternoon, golden hour, dusk, a short bright night, and first light pass in about twelve minutes of active play. The day runs on the same clock as the crops, so it stops when you pause or leave and picks up at the same hour when you return. It changes the light and the sounds and nothing else: crops grow the same at any hour. As dusk comes on, the barn and windmill windows light up, the tractor's lamps come on, and fireflies gather over the pond. Birdsong thins towards evening, crickets take over after dark with an occasional owl, and the birds return at first light. **Settings → Time of day** keeps morning, golden hour, dusk, or night for as long as you like; **Drifting** lets the day move again from there.
- **The wind plays your field.** Every minute or so a gust crosses the field from the barn side to the windmill side. You see it bend the crops and grass as it passes, and with sound on you hear it: each crop is a voice and each 2.5 m lane across the field, one pass of the tractor, is a pitch, higher towards the far side. Wheat is a soft held note, sunflowers a small bell, pumpkins a low wooden tone on every other beat, and stubble a quiet brush. Seeds are silent, young crops are faint, and bare soil and grass are rests, so a long row is a long note and a gap is a pause. After dark the same field plays in a minor key. There is nothing to get right; any field sounds pleasant. In the browser each plant bends as the gust reaches it; in the native app the field leans patch by patch.
- **Meadow postcards.** The camera button makes a picture of your meadow exactly as it stands, at whatever hour it is, on a cream card with a line about what is growing. In the browser, choose the whole farm or a closer view of just the field, which shows off patterns you have mown and sown. In the native app the postcard shows your current view, so zoom or switch to tractor view first to frame it. Save it, or send it with your device's share sheet where there is one. The picture is made on your device and is not uploaded anywhere.
- Ground states, selected crop, exact growth progress, mown paths, harvest displays, tractor position, tool, the hour of the day, and settings save on this device. There is no account or cloud sync.
- **Settings → A fresh meadow** asks before clearing the field. The rest of your preferences stay in place.
